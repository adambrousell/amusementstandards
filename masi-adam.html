SCORING & METHODOLOGY
"How do you score a facility? What's the methodology?"
BUILT
HIGH
RIDE Score is a weighted composite across 4 pillars — Readiness, Integrity, Delivery, Environment — fed by 15 data streams. Scoring methodology is published at the 10,000-foot and 30,000-foot level on amusementstandards.com. Weights are configurable per facility type. Parallel scores exist for aquatics (SPLASH Score, 12 streams, 6 pillars) and FECs (PLAY Score, 10 streams). Total architecture: 37 data streams across the three indices.
Anchor on the FICO analogy — "We're building the credit score for ride safety. Just like FICO aggregates payment history, credit utilization, and length of history into a single number, RIDE Score aggregates inspection compliance, maintenance velocity, operator behavior, and incident patterns."
"How is the score validated? Is it just self-reported data?"
BUILT
HIGH
Multiple validation layers. First, behavioral integrity analysis flags statistical anomalies in self-reported data — checklist speed, zero-variance patterns, submission clustering. Second, an independent external crowdsourced validation stream provides guest-side audit data that pipes into AS 360 as an external signal. The external platform is structurally independent from operator-facing tools so the data can't be influenced. Third, the amendment system preserves immutable original records — any corrections create linked amendments with geo-verification, device binding, and supervisor approval.
Don't name AmusementRating.org — just say "We have an independent, structurally separated external validation platform." The independence is the selling point. If they ask more, explain the architecture without revealing the brand.
"Can we customize scoring weights for our book of business?"
ROADMAP
MED
Architecture supports configurable weights per insurer. The 4-pillar structure (R/I/D/E) stays constant, but the weight allocation between pillars can be adjusted based on what an insurer prioritizes. For example, MASI might weight Integrity at 35% vs. the default 25% because audit integrity is your primary loss control concern. Implementation: 4-6 weeks for insurer-specific weight profiles.
Frame as: "The framework is fixed — that's what makes it comparable across facilities. But the emphasis is configurable. Think of it like credit scoring where the categories are standard but the model can be tuned for different lending products."

"Can you detect whether inspections are being fabricated?"
DEMO
HIGH
Three anomaly detection patterns are built into AuditScore: (1) Speed flags — checklist completed significantly faster than peer average, suggesting incomplete review. (2) Zero-variance flags — consecutive days of 100% PASS on multi-item checklists, which is statistically improbable. (3) Clustering flags — multiple checklists submitted in rapid sequence, often just before supervisor arrival. These are presented to managers as integrity flags and to insurers as behavioral compliance risk signals.
This is your single biggest differentiator. Every competitor has a checklist app. Nobody else detects whether the checklists are real. Lead with the L. Thompson example — 14 consecutive days all PASS, completing in 34 seconds vs. 4.1 minute peer average. That's the story Noah needs to hear.
"What happens when someone needs to correct an inspection record?"
DEMO
HIGH
Audit Amendment Request (AMR) system. Operator submits a correction request that includes the original value, the requested correction, and a written justification. The system verifies: (1) request originated from same geo-fenced location as original, (2) same device that submitted original, (3) optional photo evidence. Original record is sealed and immutable. Supervisor has 4 hours to approve or deny. Pattern detection flags operators with frequent amendment requests.
The immutability angle is what makes this different from "just editing a field." Show the AMR queue in the AuditScore tab — the ORIGINAL → REQUESTED visual with geo/device/photo verification badges.
INCIDENT & RISK MANAGEMENT
"How do you capture and track incidents and near-misses?"
BUILT
HIGH
Two primary forms: IRL (Incident Report Log) for actual incidents and NMR (Near-Miss Report) for close calls. Both feed into Airtable with severity classification, ride association, and timestamp. CARs (Corrective Action Records) are auto-generated from incidents and track resolution with aging metrics. CACR (Corrective Action Closure Rate) is a primary scoring input. The escalation matrix defines response protocols by severity level.
"Can you monitor radio traffic for unreported incidents?"
DEMO
MED
VoxIntel is a passive radio intelligence layer — RTL-SDR scanner captures unencrypted analog FM radio traffic, runs it through VAD → Whisper → NLP classification → structured event log. Classifies events as ride-down, medical, EMS, maintenance, queue, security. Cross-references against reported incidents to detect unreported events. Currently demo with simulated feed; hardware deployment scoped at a proof-of-concept site ~7 miles from a major facility.
Be transparent: "VoxIntel is our most forward-looking capability. We have the demo feed running in the Manager Module, hardware is scoped, and we have a PoC deployment site. Production deployment is a Phase 2 deliverable." Don't oversell — but do show the feed. The concept alone is powerful.

"Can you ingest our claims data and loss control reports?"
PLANNED
HIGH
Yes — and this is where the platform becomes most valuable to MASI specifically. Under mutual NDA, AS 360 can ingest: (1) Claims history — frequency, severity, type, and total incurred by facility, enabling claims-to-score correlation analysis. (2) Loss control visit reports — findings, remediation status, and follow-up schedules, displayed alongside AS 360 inspection data so you can see if your recommendations are actually being implemented. (3) Historical loss runs — for backtest analysis correlating RIDE Score trajectories with claims outcomes. (4) Underwriting notes — premium history and coverage terms for renewal decision support. All MASI data is encrypted at rest (AES-256) and in transit (TLS 1.3), stored in a tenant-isolated partition, and accessible only to authorized MASI personnel. The data never touches operator-facing systems — complete firewall between operational and insurer data.
This is a high-priority conversation starter. Lead with: "The most powerful thing we can do together is correlate your claims history against our operational scores. OPERATOR INTELLIGENCE & DATA
"Can you track operator performance and training compliance?"
BUILT
HIGH
OpsScan 3-tier model: Tier 1 passively ingests existing shift management data (Kronos/ADP/Mobaro), Tier 2 adds CV-based position tracking from existing CCTV, Tier 3 adds active QR scan-in and voice logging. Rotation tracking, staffing compliance, position coverage gaps, and training module completion (SCORM 1.2 packages via TalentLMS) all feed into scoring. The AuditScore gamification engine tracks per-operator XP, streaks, and completion rates.
