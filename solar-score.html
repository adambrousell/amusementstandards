Scoring framework

SOLAR Score methodology

Five pillars. Twelve data streams. One composite score on a 0–100 scale.

S
Structure
20% of composite
  • Racking integrity score (IEC 62446-1 §8 compliance)
  • Mounting torque verification (last inspection date + pass/fail)
  • Ballast compliance status (roof-mounted systems)
  • Penetration seal condition (water intrusion risk)
O
Operation
25% of composite
  • O&M vendor certification tier (NABCEP-certified vs uncertified)
  • Monitoring system uptime % (trailing 90 days)
  • Work order open/close ratio (open WOs older than 30 days flagged)
  • Mean time to respond on priority alerts (hours)
L
Legal
15% of composite
  • AHJ permit documentation status (current / expired / missing)
  • NEC Article 690 compliance last verified (date + inspector credential)
  • Utility interconnection agreement current (yes/no + expiration date)
A
Asset
25% of composite
  • Performance ratio vs P50 baseline (trailing 12 months)
  • Inverter fault frequency (faults per MW per quarter)
  • IEC 62446-3 thermographic inspection recency (months since last IR scan)
  • String-level degradation vs expected (% deviation from nameplate)
R
Risk
15% of composite
  • Climate hazard exposure index (hail, wildfire, wind — zip code mapped)
  • Vegetation management compliance (last clearance date vs standard)
  • Insurance claim history normalized (claims per MW per 5-year period)
  • Site access road condition (emergency response access graded 1–5)

Operation and Asset carry the highest weights (25% each) because insurer loss data identifies O&M negligence and inverter failure as the primary drivers of attritional losses in the commercial solar segment. Structure and Risk carry equal weight (20% and 15%) reflecting the importance of physical integrity and nat-cat exposure in the commercial rooftop context, where roof membrane damage from racking failure is a significant liability pathway.

Legal carries 15% — not because compliance is unimportant, but because AHJ and NEC documentation failures are typically curable within 30 days and should not permanently suppress a facility's composite score.

The SOLAR Score is not a pass/fail compliance tool. It is a composite risk signal that incorporates standards-based inspection data alongside operational telemetry and documentation status. Facilities that meet IEC 62446 documentation requirements but demonstrate poor monitoring uptime or chronic work order backlogs will score accordingly. The composite captures operational reality — not just paperwork compliance.

Longitudinal signal

Score trajectory — the longitudinal advantage

A single SOLAR Score is a snapshot. Two scores separated by 6–12 months are a trend. Three scores are actuarial evidence. The SOLAR Score is designed to be scored on a semi-annual cadence, building a longitudinal operational risk dataset — the first of its kind for the commercial solar segment.

Improving

Improving trajectory → preferred tier

A facility that moves from WATCH to GOOD to STRONG across three scoring cycles demonstrates documented operational improvement — qualifying for enhanced underwriting terms at renewal.

Stable

Flat trajectory → standard renewal

A facility that maintains GOOD across multiple cycles with no significant pillar deterioration represents stable, predictable risk — priced accordingly.

Declining

Declining trajectory → early warning

A facility whose score drops across two consecutive cycles signals emerging operational risk before a claim event occurs. Insurers can condition renewal, require engineering review, or adjust deductibles proactively.

The analogy to consumer credit scoring is intentional. A FICO score is not useful because it captures a moment — it is useful because lenders have decades of longitudinal data correlating score trajectory with default probability. The SOLAR Score is building that same dataset for solar asset operational risk. Early adopters — both asset owners and insurers — benefit from being part of the foundational data set.

Engagement

Pilot program — Solar Landscape

Score 5 assets. 30 days. No operational disruption.

1
Data intake Days 1–5

Solar Landscape shares existing O&M reports, monitoring platform exports, permit documentation, and interconnection agreements for 5 designated facilities. AS 360 executes a Data Processing Agreement before intake.

2
Remote scoring Days 6–18

AS 360 scores each facility against all 12 SOLAR Score streams using submitted documentation, public AHJ records, NOAA climate hazard data, and satellite vegetation imagery. No site visits required for initial scoring cycle.

3
Gap analysis Days 19–22

Each facility receives a pillar-level gap report identifying the specific streams driving score suppression. Corrective action recommendations are prioritized by insurance impact, not by remediation cost.

4
Insurer-ready reporting Days 23–27

Five score reports formatted for submission to Solar Landscape's insurance broker. Reports include composite score, pillar breakdown, standards alignment status, and trajectory projection.

5
BD presentation Day 30

AS 360 presents pilot findings to Tom Coyne and the Solar Landscape BD team. Discussion: how the SOLAR Score integrates into Solar Landscape's client onboarding, asset management workflow, and insurer relationship.

Solar Landscape owns 800MW of rooftop assets across 20 states. There is no standardized operational risk score on any of them. The pilot changes that — in 30 days, at zero cost to operations.
