Session Abstract

A New Framework for Quantifying Operational Risk

When a lender evaluates credit risk, they don't just look at whether you paid your last bill. They analyze behavioral patterns over time — utilization, history, trajectory — and produce a composite score that predicts future risk, not just reflects past performance.

The attractions industry doesn't have this yet. Specialty insurers writing ride liability today largely rely on loss history, self-reported applications, and periodic inspections. That's the equivalent of approving a mortgage based on whether the applicant says they pay their bills on time.

This session introduces a composite operational risk scoring framework — built from inspection compliance, maintenance documentation, operator training metrics, and guest experience signals — that gives operators, insurers, and regulators a leading indicator of safety performance, not a lagging one. Attendees will learn how data streams already present in most operations can be structured into a standardized, quantifiable score, and why the attractions industry is overdue for this transformation.

Learning Objectives

What Attendees Will Walk Away With

01
Understand why composite risk scoring works in other industries (credit, workers' comp, auto insurance) and what's required to apply the same model to attractions safety.
02
Identify the operational data streams already present in most facilities — inspection records, maintenance logs, training completion, incident reports — and how they can be structured into measurable risk indicators.
03
Apply a practical pillar-based framework (Readiness, Integrity, Delivery, Environment) for evaluating operational risk across different attraction types — dry rides, aquatics, and FEC environments.
04
Recognize how demonstrating quantified safety posture can influence insurance pricing, regulatory relationships, and competitive positioning within the market.

Why This Talk

The Industry Gap

Every year, over 400 million ride cycles occur at U.S. amusement parks and attractions. The specialty insurance market covering these operations exceeds $5 billion. Yet there is no standardized, data-driven framework for quantifying operational safety risk across the industry.

Other sectors solved this decades ago. Credit scoring transformed lending. Telematics transformed auto insurance. AI-driven workplace safety platforms are now transforming workers' compensation. The attractions industry — with its complex mix of mechanical systems, seasonal workforces, aquatic environments, and guest behavior variables — is the next frontier.

This session doesn't sell a product. It makes the case that the industry needs a common language for operational risk, and walks through what building that language actually looks like — the data streams, the scoring architecture, and the stakeholders who benefit.
