The Problem

A single audit is a snapshot.
Your risk is a moving picture.

Most facilities rely on one data source at a time. That leaves gaps that incidents find first.

01

Audits capture one day

Your team performs well during a scheduled visit. But what about Tuesday afternoon when the regional manager isn't watching? A single audit tells you what happened on that date. It doesn't tell you what's happening now.

02

Data lives in silos

Safety audits in one binder. Guest complaints in another system. Maintenance logs somewhere else. Near-misses in nobody's system. Without connecting these signals, patterns stay hidden until something breaks.

03

No way to measure confidence

When your internal audit says 85% but guests are complaining online, which number do you trust? Without a way to measure whether your data sources agree with each other, you're guessing which signal is real.

How It Works

Six independent data streams.
One trusted score.

The RIDE Score doesn't rely on any single source. It cross-references multiple independent signals and measures how much they agree — because agreement between independent sources is the closest thing to truth.

What Makes It Different

We don't just score you.
We measure how much we trust the score.

Every RIDE Score comes with a convergence indicator — a measure of how much the independent data streams agree. High convergence means you can trust the number. Low convergence means something is being masked.

Convergence in action

Imagine your field audit scores dispatch at 85%. But live queue data shows 45-minute average waits with six rides down. And near-miss reports in the dispatch category are spiking.

A traditional audit would hand you the 85% and call it a day. The RIDE Score flags the disagreement. Your Readiness score adjusts downward, and the convergence indicator tells you: "The data doesn't agree here — investigate."

That signal is worth more than any single number.
