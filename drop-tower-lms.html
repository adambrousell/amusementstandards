Module 1: Ride Systems & Components

Unit 1 · Foundations · ~12 min

ℹ️
ASTM F1193 requires manufacturers to provide documented operating instructions for every ride. Your supervisor should have provided the ride-specific SOP (Standard Operating Procedure). This module supplements — not replaces — that documentation.
🎡 Drop Tower Overview

A drop tower (also called a free-fall, gyro-drop, or tower ride) consists of a gondola or seat assembly that is mechanically raised to height, then released in a controlled downward motion before braking brings the gondola safely to rest. Understanding the ride's mechanical logic is essential to recognizing abnormal operation — one of your core responsibilities as a certified operator.

Free-Fall (Gravity Drop)
Gondola rises via electromagnetic or hydraulic lift, releases at apex for a brief free-fall, then magnetic or eddy-current brakes engage progressively. Most common configuration at major parks.
Gyro Drop
Gondola rises while rotating outward, then drops. The rotational component adds centrifugal force that affects restraint verification and requires specific pre-ride body posture checks.
Turbo Drop / Shot Tower
Uses compressed air or hydraulic launch to drive gondola upward before free-fall. Launch pressure systems require specific daily pressure and fluid checks beyond standard inspection.
Space Shot
Rapid upward launch from ground level to apex then free-fall return. Hydraulic cylinders and launch pressure components require additional monitoring in daily inspection cycle.
⚙️ Major System Components

Every drop tower shares these critical systems. You are required to visually inspect each one during your pre-opening check. Develop a mental map of each component and its location on your specific ride.

🔐 Restraint System CRITICAL
Over-the-shoulder restraints (OTSR) or lap bars with positive-lock mechanism. Requires manual physical verification of each seat every cycle. Sensor confirmation is a secondary check — never rely solely on indicators.
🔧 Lift / Drive Mechanism CRITICAL
Motor, chain, cable, or hydraulic cylinder system that raises the gondola. Any unusual sounds (grinding, squeaking, popping), vibrations, or fluid leaks must halt operations and trigger immediate maintenance notification.
🛑 Braking System CRITICAL
Eddy current, magnetic, or hydraulic brakes governing descent control. Service brakes, emergency brakes, and anti-rollback devices all require daily functional testing per ASTM F770 §7.1.1.6.
🖥️ Control System CRITICAL
PLC (programmable logic controller), operator console, E-stop buttons at all required positions, and communication system. All require functional testing before opening. A non-functional E-stop means the ride stays closed.
🏗️ Tower Structure INSPECT DAILY
Steel guide rails, main structural column, gondola assembly, foundation hardware, and all visible fasteners. Look for corrosion, cracking, deformation, missing bolts, or foreign objects in the travel path.
🚪 Loading Platform ROUTINE
Entry/exit gates, loading zone barriers, platform flooring, and operator safe zone boundaries. Must be clear, free of trip hazards, and gates must function correctly before first guest enters the station.
👁️ Recognizing Abnormal Operation

Over time you will develop an intuitive awareness of what normal operation looks, sounds, and smells like. When anything deviates, your obligation is clear: report it and take appropriate action. Watch for:

  • Strange Sights: Sparks, fluid leaks, metal shavings, gondola stopping at different positions than normal, components that appear misaligned
  • Unusual Smells: Burning rubber or metal, electrical smell, hydraulic fluid odor, chemical smell
  • Unfamiliar Sounds: Grinding, squeaking, thumping, popping, changes in motor tone, unusual vibration frequency
  • Guest Comments: "That hurt," "Fix that," "Something feels wrong" — take all ride-related comments seriously and report immediately
⚠️
The Golden Rule: You as a Ride Team Member have the right at any time to close the ride if you feel that an unsafe condition exists. This is not a suggestion — it is your authority and your obligation. Use it without hesitation.

Module 2: Regulatory Framework

Unit 1 · Foundations · ~10 min

📜 The Regulatory Landscape

Unlike many industries, fixed amusement parks have no federal oversight. The CPSC (Consumer Product Safety Commission) has jurisdiction only over portable rides. This means state law — and industry standards — govern your operations.

ASTM International's Committee F24 on Amusement Rides and Devices publishes the standards that 38+ U.S. states have incorporated into law. These include:

ASTM F770-18
Standard Practice for Ownership, Operation, Maintenance, and Inspection of Amusement Rides and Devices. The primary compliance document for your daily operations. This is the standard your inspection sheets, training records, and operating procedures are designed to satisfy.
ASTM F1193
Standard Practice for Quality, Manufacture, and Construction of Amusement Rides and Devices. Governs manufacturers — but creates important obligations for operators, including incident reporting timelines and the right to receive Safety Alerts from manufacturers.
§8 — Training Program Requirements

F770-18 Section 8 directly governs this training program. These are legally binding requirements:

  1. §8.1 — Written Programs Required: Owner/operators must maintain documented training programs for all operators, attendants, and maintenance staff assigned to each ride. Your completion of this LMS generates that documentation.
  2. §8.2 — Minimum Content: Training must cover: (a) operating procedures, (b) assigned position duties, (c) general safety, (d) procedures for unusual conditions and ride interruptions, (e) evacuation plan, and (f) inspection and PM procedures for maintenance staff.
  3. §8.3 — Supervised Demonstration (REQUIRED): The trainer must first demonstrate all assigned duties. Then you must demonstrate competency under trainer supervision. This LMS course alone does NOT fulfill §8.3. A field sign-off from your supervisor is mandatory before solo operation.
§7 — Inspection Program Requirements

§7.1 Pre-Opening Inspection
Daily documented and signed inspection prior to carrying riders. Covers all 9 elements detailed in Module 3. Your signature is a legal certification of compliance.

§7.2 Pre-Opening Test Cycle
Minimum one complete operating cycle without riders before opening each day. This is mandatory — not optional and not waivable by any supervisor.

§7.3 Return to Service Protocol
After any unscheduled stop (malfunction, adjustment, modification, environmental condition), the ride must be re-inspected and operated without riders for a minimum of one full cycle before carrying guests again.

§7.4 Documentation
All inspection records must be maintained per F770 and F1193. These records are accessible to state inspectors, and falsification constitutes fraud.

§10 — Incident Reporting Obligations
  • Serious injury notification: Under F770 §10.3, the manufacturer must be notified within 7 days of any incident resulting in serious injury. Your supervisor handles this, but your accurate incident documentation is what makes it possible.
  • First-aid log: All first-aid treatments — including minor injuries — must be recorded. Every event, no matter how minor it seems, gets documented.
  • Manufacturer Safety Alerts: Under F1193, manufacturers who learn of a critical component incident must evaluate it and issue a Safety Alert, Service Bulletin, or Notification to all known operators. Stay current with any bulletins applicable to your ride.
🚨
Your Legal Exposure: When you sign a daily inspection sheet, you are certifying compliance under penalty of fraud. Falsifying safety records may result in termination, civil liability, and potential criminal prosecution. If you cannot complete an inspection item, notify your supervisor BEFORE opening — never after a problem occurs.

Module 3: Pre-Opening Inspection Protocol

Unit 2 · Daily Operations · ~15 min

⚠️
ASTM F770-18 §7.1: "Prior to carrying riders, the owner/operator shall conduct or cause to be conducted a daily documented and signed preopening inspection." Maintenance must sign off first. You may never open a ride without their signature on the Ride Operations Inspection Checklist.
🔑 Maintenance Sign-Off First

Each morning, Maintenance will conduct a thorough inspection and test operation of the ride. At the conclusion, the Maintenance technician signs off on both the daily maintenance inspection report AND the Ride Operations Inspection Checklist. You may not start the ride without this signature.

"Ride operators are trained to never start any ride without a signature from Maintenance on the Ride Operations Inspection Checklist." If you arrive and the sheet is incomplete or unsigned, contact your Supervisor immediately for direction. Do not open the ride.

✅ ASTM F770 §7.1.1 — 9-Point Inspection Checklist

The following 9 elements are mandatory per §7.1.1. Use this interactive checklist to practice the protocol before your practical assessment. All 9 must be checked before signing off.

🔍 Common Defects & Required Responses
⛔ Restraint Will Not Lock CLOSE SEAT / LOTO
Remove that seat from service via LOTO. Notify maintenance immediately. Do not carry riders in that seat. If multiple seats are affected, the ride may not open until maintenance evaluates and signs off.
⛔ E-Stop Non-Functional DO NOT OPEN
A non-functional emergency stop at any required position means the ride is closed. Contact maintenance immediately. Document the time of discovery. No supervisor may authorize opening with a failed E-stop.
⚠️ Unusual Sound During Test Cycle NOTIFY MAINT
Describe the sound's nature, location, and when in the cycle it occurs. Contact maintenance before opening. Do not open until maintenance evaluates and provides written sign-off.
⚠️ Missing or Damaged Signage REPLACE FIRST
Height restriction and rider requirement signs are safety-critical. Guests rely on these to self-screen. Obtain replacement signage before opening. Document the condition and time of replacement.
🔄 Pre-Opening Test Cycle (§7.2)

After completing and signing your inspection checklist, perform a minimum of one complete operating cycle without riders:

  1. Confirm all required attendant positions are staffed and in position
  2. Announce test cycle on communication system if your SOP requires it
  3. Operate ride through the full cycle: observe loading zone, lift, apex, drop, brake sequence, and unload return
  4. Listen and watch throughout — any abnormality during the test cycle is a stop-and-investigate situation
  5. Confirm gondola returns to correct load position and all systems reset to ready state
  6. Complete and sign inspection documentation. The ride is not open until you sign.

Module 4: Rider Screening & Loading

Unit 2 · Daily Operations · ~15 min

📏 Height & Size Requirements: Zero Exceptions
⛔
Zero Exceptions Policy: "Even a Guest only 1/8 of an inch under the requirement is NOT ALLOWED to ride. Again there are NO exceptions." This applies regardless of parental pressure, emotional appeals, claims of prior rides elsewhere, or any other circumstance. Your signature on the dispatch is your legal certification of compliance.

Height Checking Procedure:

  1. Guest stands erect with feet together, heels flat on the ground, looking straight ahead
  2. No tip-toes. Shoes remain on (unless heel height >1" and measurement is borderline)
  3. Head must be on or above the minimum height indicator line
  4. Use the Official Height Stick for borderline cases — not the estimation device
  5. If a Guest cannot ride, direct them to rides they ARE eligible for — always offer a positive alternative
⚠️
When a Guest claims "I rode this before": "If the Guest does not meet the requirement at the time of boarding, then they are not allowed to ride." Prior experience at this park or any other park is irrelevant. Different shoes or a different day's measurement do not authorize boarding below the requirement today.
🏥 Health Restriction Screening

Health restrictions are posted at ride entrance. You are not required to diagnose conditions, but when a guest discloses a restriction-listed condition, you must deny boarding. Common drop tower restrictions include:

  • Heart conditions or cardiac disease
  • High blood pressure (uncontrolled)
  • Back, neck, or spinal conditions
  • Recent surgery (typically within 6 months)
  • Seizure disorders or epilepsy
  • Pregnancy (all trimesters)
  • Osteoporosis or bone density conditions
  • Under the influence of alcohol or drugs
  • Extreme fear of heights (guest welfare)
  • Prosthetics or medical devices (SOP-specific)

"Only the person with the disability knows their ability and limitations." If any doubt exists about whether a guest can safely ride, contact your Supervisor for guidance.

🔐 Restraint Verification — Every Seat, Every Cycle

This is the most critical step before dispatch. Position accountability is clearly assigned:

Attendant Role
Check height. Check security of restraint devices. Secure gates. Give proper hand signal. Assist in loading/unloading. Enforce proper body posture. Perform the Take 5 safety scan of your zone.
Operator Role
Deliver spiels. Receive proper hand signals from ALL attendants. Perform the Take 5 safety scan. The operator does NOT dispatch until all clear signals are received. The operator is the LAST safety check.
  1. Body posture check: Rider seated correctly per SOP. Arms, legs, and loose items in required position before restraint is lowered.
  2. Physical restraint engagement: Confirm OTSR clicks/locks positively OR lap bar is within the approved engagement range. Never rely on the rider's word — physically verify every restraint every cycle.
  3. Size verification: OTSR must not bottom out (indicates rider too small) and must not be too loose (rider too large). Lap bar must engage within the approved measurement range per your ride's SOP.
  4. Loose articles check: No items in seat, lap, or pockets. No loose headwear, dangling jewelry, or items that could become projectiles at speed.
  5. Hand signal: After verifying your assigned zone, give the correct "All Clear" signal (right hand extended above head, thumb extended upward). Only signal from your designated Safe Zone.
🔒 Loose Articles Program

"Loose articles can be projectiles and can cause great harm, especially cameras and cell phones." Items that cannot be secured in the restraint area may not be taken on the ride. Common loose articles requiring action:

  • Food and beverages (not permitted on ride)
  • Hats and headwear (not secured by restraint)
  • Glasses (unless secured with strap)
  • Phones, cameras, wallets, keys
  • Backpacks and bags
  • Extremely long, loose scarves or ponchos
  • Stuffed animals and packages
  • Child harnesses that could interfere with restraint
👋 Hand Signals Reference

Hand signals provide a redundant safety communication channel that works when audio fails. Memorize all signals before your practical assessment. Key signals for drop tower operation:

✅ All Clear / Dispatch: Right hand extended above head, thumb extended upward. Given only from Safe Zone, only when zone is fully verified ready. Operator waits for this signal from ALL attendants before dispatching.

🛑 Stop / Hold: Both arms crossed in front of chest or raised overhead with fists clenched. Use verbal "STOP" when possible. Non-emergency only.

⚡ Emergency Stop: Open palm over a clenched fist. Use with verbal "E-STOP!" Applies to mid-cycle emergencies only — last resort.

🔓 Unlock Restraints: Both fists clenched, thumbs extended pointing outward, bottom of fists touching. Signal to attendants to unlock restraints for unloading.

🔒 Lock Restraints: Both fists clenched, thumbs pointing inward and touching. Signal that restraints should now be engaged for dispatch.

🆘 Assistance Needed: One arm extended overhead, open palm forward. All ride operations cease immediately when this signal is given. Medical or security situation.

⛔
The Golden Rule: "The operator is responsible for starting or dispatching the ride. Therefore they are the last safety check to ensure all is ready to go. If the ride operator feels that any safety checks were not done in the proper method, they should not start the ride or dispatch."

Module 5: LOTO & Operator Safe Zones

Unit 3 · Safety & Emergency · ~12 min

🚨
Lock Out / Tag Out is a life-saving procedure. The purpose is to prevent any part of machinery from being energized, set into motion, or started unexpectedly. A failure of LOTO protocol is a potential fatality event — for you, for maintenance staff, or for guests.
🔒 When Is LOTO Required?

LOTO is required in all of the following situations:

  • During maintenance inspection checks or when doing repair work on the ride or any machine component
  • Whenever entering a restricted ride perimeter or danger zone — for example, when retrieving lost articles
  • If a guest or team member is ill or injured and movement of the ride could endanger them
  • To close or secure a specific seat, unit, or the entire ride
  • When utilizing standard evacuation procedures of a stopped ride
⛔
Absolute Rule — No Exceptions: "You may NEVER remove another Team Member's lock out/tag out from a ride or any other machine." Only the person whose name appears on the lock/tag may remove it. Removing someone else's LOTO device could injure or kill whoever placed it. This is a terminable offense subject to OSHA criminal penalties.
📋 What To Do When You Find a Lock at Your Ride

If you arrive at your assigned ride and find a Lock Out/Tag Out device on the control panel or equipment:

  1. Do not attempt to start the ride under any circumstances
  2. Read the tag — note whose name appears on the lock
  3. Look around the area for the tagged team member — they may be in the ride area performing work
  4. If you cannot locate the personnel, call your Supervisor immediately
  5. Do not remove the lock — do not call the person and ask permission to remove their lock
  6. Document the time you discovered the lock and who you contacted
🏃 Restricted Areas, Danger Zones & Safe Zones

There are three escalating zones of restriction of restriction around every ride. As an operator, you must be able to identify all three on your assigned ride:

Ride Perimeter
Boundary fence encompassing the ride's entire footprint. Must be 42" minimum height, meet the 4" ball requirement, have locked gate(s), and have safety signage. Area inside is restricted to authorized personnel only.
Ride Restricted Area
Inside the perimeter, excluding the station and authorized walkways. Off-limits without direct supervisor approval. Entry requires the ride to be locked out AND wearing a safety orange vest.
⚠️ Danger Zone EXTREME HAZARD
The clearance envelope surrounding all moving parts. Requires LOTO, supervisor present, AND safety vest. "Entering a danger zone without proper authorization may result in being struck by the ride causing a serious catastrophic injury or death."
Operator Safe Zone
A designated area at each position from which attendants must operate when the ride is moving. Safe zones are marked at each position. You must be inside your safe zone when giving the All Clear signal and during all ride movement in your area.
⛔
If you see a person in a danger zone during operation: Stop the ride IMMEDIATELY by pressing the nearest E-Stop. Do NOT enter the area. Attempt to communicate for them to stay flat and still. Notify Supervisor and Security immediately.

Module 6: Emergency Procedures

Unit 3 · Safety & Emergency · ~15 min

📞
Memorize Before Your First Shift: Emergency Dispatch: Extension 6 | Security/First Aid Emergency: ext. 5 | Security Non-Emergency: ext. 2655 | First Aid Non-Emergency: ext. 2655. Post these at your operator station.
⚡ Ride Downtime Classification

A downtime is any unplanned ride stoppage — but not necessarily an emergency. Downtimes are classified into two types that determine the required response:

Operations Downtime
A stoppage under controlled conditions, quickly resolved without Maintenance sign-off. Examples: guest misbehavior (standing), weather (lightning, heavy rain), guest illness, open restraint, restricted area violation. Guests can typically be unloaded in the normal unloading area.
Maintenance Downtime MAINT REQUIRED
Unanticipated closing requiring Maintenance Personnel and their written sign-off to resolve. Examples: unusual noise or function, ride control system fault, restraint that will not lock, power outage, equipment malfunction. Ride may NOT reopen without Maintenance sign-off.
🔄 Ride Downtime Protocol — Step by Step
  1. All team members at the ride remain at their positions. Do NOT rotate or go on break without Supervisor approval.
  2. Call Dispatch (Extension 6) immediately. Provide: your name, ride name, reason (if known), locations of any loaded units.
  3. Record the downtime on the back of the turnstile.
  4. The Certified Ride Operator remains at the operator position to host guests in station or bullpen.
  5. Notify the ride entrance that the ride is experiencing minor technical difficulty and is temporarily closed.
  6. If the ride must be unloaded, an Operations Supervisor must be present. Call Dispatch to notify of the unload.
  7. If evacuation is required, Ride Supervisors and Operations Management make that decision — not the operator team alone (except in cases of immediate danger).
  8. When the ride reopens: Supervisor tells you it's ready, call Dispatch (ext. 2113), record re-open time on turnstile.
🚨 Emergency Downtime: Immediate Guest Danger
⚠️
"If Guests are in immediate danger, do not wait for the Supervisor — SHUT DOWN THE RIDE." Your authority to act protectively is absolute in a genuine emergency.

If guests are stopped at height and in distress:

  1. Communicate first: Use the intercom immediately — calm, clear message. "Please remain seated with your restraints on. We have temporarily paused the ride and will update you shortly." A panicked guest attempting to self-evacuate at height is a fatal risk.
  2. Activate E-Stop if not already triggered
  3. Call Dispatch (Ext. 6) and Supervisor simultaneously
  4. Assess: Is evacuation required? Are guests in immediate danger? Can the cause be quickly resolved?
  5. Evacuation requires Supervisor authorization unless guests are in immediate life-threatening danger
  6. During evacuation: ride must be locked out first. Follow ride-specific evacuation plan exactly — do not improvise.
⛔
Never improvise evacuation. Your ride-specific evacuation plan was engineered by safety professionals. Unauthorized improvisation during evacuation is a leading cause of secondary injuries. "During the evacuation never make comments regarding the possible reason for the ride breakdown."
🏥 Guest Injury Response Protocol
  1. Activate E-Stop. Do not attempt to restart or assess the situation before stopping the ride.
  2. Call First Aid (ext. 5) and Security simultaneously — do not assess severity first and then call. Call immediately.
  3. Do NOT move an injured guest unless there is imminent danger of further injury (fire, structural collapse).
  4. Keep bystanders — guests and unauthorized staff — away from the scene. Preserve the area exactly as found.
  5. Do not discuss the incident with witnesses, guests, or media. Provide facts only to First Aid, Security, and park management.
  6. Complete a detailed incident report as soon as possible while your memory is fresh and uninfluenced.

Under ASTM F770 §10.3: any incident resulting in serious injury requires manufacturer notification within 7 days. Your supervisor handles the notification — but your accurate documentation enables it.

🔥 Fire Emergency Protocol
  1. Activate the nearest fire alarm pull station if available
  2. Call Security immediately — ext. 5
  3. Evacuate guests from the ride and immediate area using the nearest designated exit route
  4. You are NOT required to fight the fire — your priority is guest evacuation and safety
  5. Only use a fire extinguisher if: the fire is small and contained, does NOT block your egress, and you are trained in its use
  6. Account for all guests and team members in your area — report to emergency responders immediately upon their arrival
🌩️ Weather Procedures

Ride closures for weather are decided by Operations Management — not individual operators. However:

  • If guests are in immediate danger from weather (lightning strike imminent, structural damage visible), shut down the ride immediately without waiting for authorization
  • During severe weather downtimes, allow guests to remain in covered areas of the queue or station. Do not allow guests to wander in the ride or backstage area.
  • Never offer details or speculation about whether or when the ride will reopen — only that it is temporarily closed
