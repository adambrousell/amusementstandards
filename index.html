How the Scores Interact

Each score stands alone.
Together they close every blind spot.

Most parks operate across multiple risk domains simultaneously. A hybrid park — coasters and water slides and an FEC floor — has three separate risk profiles that a single-score approach cannot see. Here's what each score contributes and where they cross-inform.

RIDE Score
What It Sees
Operator dispatch compliance, restraint check consistency, maintenance work order closure, service bulletin status, and the full guest experience from queue entry to exit ramp. The most established index in the trilogy — 15 streams, four pillars, built for the highest-consequence asset class in your park.
Unique Contributions to the PIC
  • Mechanical integrity and manufacturer compliance signals
  • Dispatch procedure adherence as a leading safety indicator
  • Incident root cause patterns at the individual attraction level
  • Third-party audit independence — operators can't self-report their way to a high score
SPLASH Score
What It Sees
Lifeguard zone compliance, water chemistry continuity, VGB drain entrapment adherence, bather load management, and emergency action plan currency. SPLASH covers the highest-fatality-risk environment in the industry — and the one most underserved by existing risk frameworks.
Unique Contributions to the PIC
  • Lifeguard scanning zone compliance — the gap no other index quantifies
  • Water chemistry trend history — lagging indicator for RWI risk
  • VGB compliance status as a regulatory and carrier hard-stop signal
  • Health department inspection record as a third-party validation stream
PLAY Score
What It Sees
FEC throughput performance, liability documentation quality, mixed-use attraction condition, and — critically — the Youth pillar: height enforcement, unaccompanied minor policies, birthday group supervision, and child-specific incident patterns. PLAY is the only score in the industry that treats minor patron risk as a standalone scored dimension.
Unique Contributions to the PIC
  • Youth pillar — quantifying minor patron safeguards no other index measures
  • Liability documentation score as a litigation-readiness proxy
  • Attraction uptime and throughput yield as an operational risk signal
  • Mixed-use incident pattern analysis across heterogeneous attraction types
Cross-Score Intelligence — How the Scores Inform Each Other
RIDE → PLAY
Ride incident patterns and dispatch compliance trends from RIDE calibrate the Attraction pillar baseline in PLAY — a park with weak dispatch culture on its coasters almost always shows the same pattern on its go-karts.
SPLASH → RIDE
SPLASH's emergency action plan currency and first aid readiness scores surface into RIDE's Readiness pillar when both are active — a park that tests its EAP for aquatic incidents is more prepared for ride emergencies too.
PLAY → SPLASH
PLAY's Youth pillar data — particularly unaccompanied minor protocols and age enforcement — directly informs SPLASH's Lifeguard pillar weighting in hybrid parks where the aquatic zone is also a primary child-patron environment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about Amusement Standards

Everything you need to know about our services, the AS 360 scoring platform, and how RIDE, SPLASH, and PLAY work together.

What are the RIDE, SPLASH, and PLAY Scores — and how do they fit together?
+
RIDE, SPLASH, and PLAY are three independent operational risk indices built on the same AS 360 methodology, each designed for a specific segment of the attractions industry. RIDE Score covers dry ride environments (amusement parks, coasters, flats). SPLASH Score covers aquatic venues (water parks, slide complexes, swim facilities). PLAY Score covers FECs and entertainment centers (go-karts, trampolines, laser tag, arcade). Each score stands alone and can be deployed independently. When all three are active, they converge into the Park Intelligence Composite (PIC) — the first whole-park risk index in the industry.
What is the Park Intelligence Composite (PIC)?
+
The Park Intelligence Composite is generated when all three AS 360 scores — RIDE, SPLASH, and PLAY — are active at the same facility. It is a weighted cross-validated composite that covers every guest-facing zone of a multi-type attraction operation. For hybrid parks — those operating both dry rides and aquatic attractions, or combining coasters with an FEC floor — the PIC is the single number that tells insurers, ownership, and regulators the whole story. No existing risk framework in the attractions industry generates anything comparable.
What is the AS 360 RIDE Score?
+
The AS 360 RIDE Score is a continuous four-pillar operational risk index for amusement parks and attractions. Fifteen independent data streams — including audits, incident records, guest feedback, and live weather — feed into four scored pillars (Readiness, Integrity, Delivery, Environment) to produce a single composite risk score updated every period and formatted for insurers and regulators.
What makes the SPLASH Score different from other aquatic safety programs?
+
SPLASH Score is the first continuous aquatic risk index built specifically for the attractions industry. Its six pillars — Safety, Protocol, Lifeguard, Aquatic, Structure, Hazard — cover the full scope of aquatic operations, including VGB drain entrapment compliance, water chemistry trend history, and — critically — the Lifeguard pillar, which quantitatively scores zone compliance, certification currency, rotation adherence, and fatigue management. Lifeguard performance is the most significant driver of aquatic fatalities and the one factor most underserved by existing safety frameworks.
Why does PLAY Score have a "Youth" pillar — and why does that matter?
+
Family entertainment centers are uniquely child-dominant environments — birthday parties, school groups, and unaccompanied minors represent a fundamentally different risk profile than adult guests at an amusement park. PLAY Score's Youth pillar scores height and age enforcement protocols, unaccompanied minor policies, birthday group supervision ratios, and child-specific incident patterns as a standalone scored dimension. No other risk index in the industry quantifies minor patron risk separately — and no other pillar in the PLAY Score drives FEC liability exposure more directly.
What types of facilities does Amusement Standards serve?
+
Amusement Standards serves amusement parks, water parks, FECs (family entertainment centers), trampoline parks, adventure parks, boardwalk venues, seasonal attractions, municipal recreation departments, and ride manufacturers across the United States.
How quickly can Amusement Standards respond to a serious incident?
+
Amusement Standards' Emergency Incident Response program deploys within 48 hours of a serious incident, providing rapid expert investigation and defensible documentation suitable for insurance adjusters, attorneys, and regulators.
Does Amusement Standards work with ride manufacturers?
+
Yes. Amusement Standards offers a Manufacturer Partnership Program providing white-labeled operator verification, OEM SOP creation, Pre-Launch Intensive on-site support, and Service Bulletin Compliance Audits — all delivered invisibly under the manufacturer's brand.
